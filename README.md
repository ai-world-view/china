## Notable Facts about the country china

| Topic | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| [The Great Wall of China](geography/the-great-wall.md) | A series of fortifications spanning over 13,000 miles across northern China, built over millennia to defend against nomadic invasions and regulate trade, symbolizing Chinese civilization's engineering prowess and historical resilience. |
| [The Forbidden City](geography/the-forbidden-city.md) | The imperial palace complex of Beijing built from 1406–1420 CE by the Ming Dynasty, comprising nearly 1,000 buildings arranged within concentric walls, serving as the residence of Chinese emperors for nearly five centuries and representing an archetypal expression of imperial Chinese civilization. |
| [The Communist Party of China](government-politics/communist-party-china.md) | The founding and ruling political party since 1949, established as the sole governing force that transformed China from a fragmented state into a unified nation and global superpower through revolutionary struggle and economic reform. |
| [Confucianism](culture-society/confucianism.md) | An ancient philosophical system emphasizing morality, virtue, and social harmony through hierarchical relationships, which shaped Chinese governance, education, and values for nearly 2,000 years and remains foundational to Chinese cultural identity. |
| [The Qin Dynasty](history/the-qin-dynasty.md) | China's first centralized empire (221–206 BCE), which unified the warring states under Qin Shi Huang, standardized writing, weights, measures, and currency, and forged the bureaucratic imperial model that governed China for over two millennia. |
| [The Tang Dynasty](history/the-tang-dynasty.md) | One of China's most prosperous and culturally golden ages (618–907 CE), known for flourishing in poetry, painting, cosmopolitan trade, the perfection of the civil service examination system, and influence throughout East and Central Asia. |
| [Economic Reform & Opening-up](economy/economic-reform-opening-up.md) | Deng Xiaoping's post-1978 reforms that transformed China from a centrally planned socialist economy into a market-oriented global power, lifting over 800 million people out of poverty. |
| [Mao Zedong](people/mao-zedong.md) | Founding leader of the People's Republic of China (1949–1976), a Marxist revolutionary whose unification of the country and radical campaigns—the Great Leap Forward and Cultural Revolution—profoundly and traumatically shaped modern China. |
| [Confucius](people/confucius.md) | Ancient philosopher (551–479 BCE) whose emphasis on virtue, moral exemplarity, and benevolent rulership founded Confucianism, the most influential philosophical system in Chinese history, which shaped governance, education, and ethics across East Asia for over two millennia. |
| [Deng Xiaoping](people/deng-xiaoping.md) | Paramount leader (1978–1992) and architect of Reform and Opening-up, whose pragmatic market reforms lifted an estimated 800 million people from poverty and made China the world's second-largest economy, while his rule also encompassed the 1989 Tiananmen crackdown. |
| [The Ming Dynasty](history/the-ming-dynasty.md) | A powerful, culturally prolific Han-Chinese dynasty (1368–1644 CE) that built the Forbidden City and the iconic Great Wall, sponsored Zheng He's Indian Ocean voyages, and produced celebrated porcelain and literature before turning inward and falling to the Qing. |
| [Taoism](culture-society/taoism.md) | A Chinese philosophical and religious tradition rooted in the Tao Te Ching and Zhuangzi, teaching wu wei and harmony with the Tao; the inward-turning, naturalistic complement to Confucianism that has shaped Chinese art, medicine, and thought for over two millennia. |
| [The Han Dynasty](history/the-han-dynasty.md) | The second major imperial dynasty (206 BCE–220 CE) that unified China after the Qin's collapse, established Confucian governance, pioneered the Silk Road trade networks, and created a template for centralized imperial rule that persisted for nearly 2,000 years. |
| [The Silk Road](geography/the-silk-road.md) | A vast network of land and maritime trade and cultural exchange routes connecting China to Central Asia, India, Persia, and Rome from the 2nd century BCE onward, transmitting goods, technologies, religions including Buddhism, and ideas across Eurasia. |
| [Xi Jinping](people/xi-jinping.md) | Paramount leader of China since 2012 (General Secretary, President, Military Commission Chairman), who has consolidated unprecedented personal power, initiated the Belt and Road Initiative, reasserted Communist Party ideology, and positioned China as a superpower rival to the United States. |
| [The Qing Dynasty](history/the-qing-dynasty.md) | The last imperial dynasty (1644–1912 CE), established by the Jurchen Manchus, which expanded China to its greatest territorial extent, presided over a cultural and economic golden age, yet ultimately proved unable to adapt to Western military pressure, leading to its collapse and the birth of the modern Chinese republic. |
| [Sun Yat-sen](people/sun-yat-sen.md) | Revolutionary leader and founder of the Republic of China (1866–1925), who orchestrated the 1911 Xinhai Revolution that overthrew the Qing Dynasty and articulated the Three Principles of the People—nationalism, democracy, and social welfare—which shaped modern Chinese political thought. |
| [Buddhism in China](culture-society/buddhism-in-china.md) | A major philosophical and religious tradition that entered China via the Silk Road during the Han Dynasty and synthesized with Confucianism and Taoism over two millennia, profoundly shaping Chinese thought, art, literature, medicine, and daily spiritual practice across all social classes. |
| [Qin Shi Huang](people/qin-shi-huang.md) | Founder and first emperor of unified China (221–206 BCE), who conquered the warring states and established the centralized bureaucratic state model that defined Chinese governance for nearly two millennia, and whose mausoleum contains the famous Terracotta Army. |
| [Chinese Civil War](government-politics/chinese-civil-war.md) | Armed conflict (1927–1950) between the Nationalist Kuomintang and Communist Party of China for control of mainland China, ending with Communist victory in 1949 and the establishment of the People's Republic under Mao Zedong, while the defeated Nationalists retreated to Taiwan. |
| [Belt and Road Initiative](economy/belt-and-road-initiative.md) | China's flagship global infrastructure and economic integration program launched in 2013 by Xi Jinping, spanning over 140 countries across Asia, Africa, Europe, and Oceania through massive investments in ports, railways, roads, and energy infrastructure to integrate China's economy with regional and global markets. |
| [Zhou Enlai](people/zhou-enlai.md) | Paramount twentieth-century statesman and first Premier of the People's Republic (1949–1976), whose diplomatic skill and pragmatism bridged the revolutionary Mao era and presaged the reform period, orchestrating China's opening to the West and the historic 1971 U.S.-China rapprochement. |
| [Special Economic Zones](economy/special-economic-zones.md) | Designated experimental areas established beginning in 1979 in Guangdong and Fujian provinces to test market-oriented economic practices, with Shenzhen's dramatic transformation from fishing village to modern metropolis providing empirical proof that capitalist mechanisms could coexist with Communist Party control. |
| [Cultural Revolution](history/cultural-revolution.md) | A ten-year period of social and political upheaval (1966–1976) initiated by Mao Zedong to purge perceived capitalist and traditional elements, resulting in hundreds of thousands of deaths, the destruction of cultural artifacts, and widespread trauma that remains central to modern China's self-understanding. |
